> [!success] Nine distinct *timing* identities, not nine damage types
> The roster's real differentiation is **how each character asks the player to
> read time**, per Pillar P2. Nobody is a reskin: Cam chains, Amos covers, Vale
> gambles, Lyra throws *and* catches, Echo fights their own magic.

> [!important] These map to a **fixed vocabulary of 8 input types**
> → [[02 - Combat Design]] §3.0. Commands are **assigned from the vocabulary**, never
> invented per move — 8 systems reused across ~70 moves, not 70 minigames.
>
> **The input type is also a difficulty selector:** with 9 characters and 3–4 active,
> a player fields the hands they have. Someone who can't do rhythm never fields
> **Lyra** and loses nothing. **This is only possible because the roster is 9.**

# Confirmed cast

## 1 — Cam
**Balanced Fighter · sword + time magic · protagonist**
*Reference: Tidus (FFX).* Player-chosen gender and pronouns.

**One-line:** The person who sees the beat before it lands — and is dying for it.

> [!tip] Cam is Vaik's answer, and his mirror
> **Vaik** = V(ictus) + **Ai** + **K**ai — a man who *named himself* the union of
> time and fate. **Kym** — what Cam becomes — *is* that union. **One declared it.
> One became it.** See [[05 - World Bible]] § Vaik.

**Backstory.** Lives in Clarus's market district with older sister **Kate**.
University student alongside **Klaus**. Ordinary, a little lazy, deeply loved.
Their gift first stirs as the **déjà-vu at Gammy's fruit stall** and manifests
fully at the Ch. 1 boss — **a green flash, and time rewinds.**

**Combat identity.** Melee backbone plus the **time-magic school** — Rewind,
Haste, Slow, Stop — all on **one shared pool** (see [[01 - Game Design Document]]
§5.2). Every rewind is a Haste not cast.
- **Action command:** *combo sequence* — chained presses extending the swing.
- The pool's growth **is** Cam's progression: the number measuring their power is
  the number measuring their fluency with time.

**Symptoms — *not* Temporal Sickness.** Cam presents as **exhausted**: dark
circles, delayed responses, trouble focusing, **easily worn out after battle**.
Irises **creep** green slowly — and **complete only at the Undo**. Cam and Klaus
know it doesn't match the disease. **The world doesn't.**
- **Victory pose:** put the exhaustion in the win screen — the party celebrates,
  **Cam sits down.** One animation swap per act. See [[05 - World Bible]].

**Arc.** Passive → active → **the choice**. From time happening *to* them, to time
answering *to* them — then either:
- **Path B (Undo):** unmade, and becomes **Kym**. Time itself.
- **Path A (Restraint):** **Ai and Kai seal the Gift.** Cam lives fully. It's still
  in them, still unlockable — but **it's Cam's decision now, not the gods'.** The
  payoff isn't survival, it's **agency**: Cam ends owning the thing that owned them.

See [[05 - World Bible]] § Cam's Gift.

reads naturally either way; avoid gendered idiom.

## 2 — Klaus
**Healer / Support · canine-folk · Cam's best friend**
*Reference: a healer with a filler nuke (Ruin, FFXIV).*

**One-line:** The friend who is researching your disease before either of you
knows you don't have it.

**Backstory.** Medic, university peer, and Cam's closest friend. Researching
**Temporal Sickness** against his professor's explicit disapproval. Working the
festival medic tent when the day breaks. Shows Cam the **looping heat-stroke
patient** — the game's first clear look at the disease. **Witnesses the green
flash**, and is the first person alive to suspect what Cam is.

**Combat identity.** Primary healer; modest magic chip damage so he's never a
dead turn.
- **Action command:** *triage hold-release* — hold, release at the peak for a full
  heal; early heals partially, late is wasted. Makes healing **tense**, which
  healing usually isn't.

**Arc.** His research is the **instrument that proves the negative.** He and Cam
know almost at once that Cam's condition is **not** Temporal Sickness — Klaus
researches it, Cam is expert by proxy. He is the one person alive who can say *this
is not that* with authority, which turns the story from a misunderstanding into an
**investigation**. Then **Echo gets infected**, and Klaus has a textbook case and an
anomaly in the same party, refusing to match. `TODO:` how far does he get before the
truth arrives?

**Relationships.** Cam (best friend, first witness). **Echo (temp)** — a live
subject for his research, walking beside him. `TODO:` his professor — a named
antagonist-adjacent figure?

## 3 — Kate
**"Cook" — Control / Support · Cam's older sister · late-game recruit**

**One-line:** The warmth the whole game is about losing.

**Backstory.** Runs a food stall; cooks like it's a love language. Sends Cam on the
errand that opens the game. Survives the festival. **Joins late** — she is the
emotional anchor, and the ending belongs to her: alone in the apartment, placing an
offering at an altar with **three figures** on it.

**Combat identity.** Buffs, debuffs, and lighter healing than Klaus — a control
piece, not a second healer.
- **Action command:** *recipe sequence* — 2–3 ordered presses; buff potency scales
  with how many land. Cooking **is** a sequence; the mechanic writes itself.
- **Out of battle:** unlocks **crafting consumables**. → *This is a real system.
  See [[01 - Game Design Document]] §5.8.*

**Arc.** `TODO` — why does she join so late, and what makes her pick up a weapon?
In the finale she never learns what her sibling did, or that the god she prays to
**is** her sibling.

## 4 — Prince Amos
**Paladin / Tank · sword & shield · royalty**

**One-line:** Insufferable, magnificent, and the only reason anyone else survives
the round.

**Backstory.** High-bravado ladies-man; **the bravado is a mask**. Introduced
leaving Whisper's tent laughing about what a "joke" the reading was — which, given
what the cards were doing that day, ages horribly. `TODO:` which realm, and its
relationship to Clarus.

**Combat identity — the roster's best mechanical idea.**
> [!important] Tanks need a reason to exist in a game with no aggro
> In a turn-based game with defensive windows, "tank" is decorative unless he can
> **take the hit for someone else.** So: Amos's signature is **Intercept** — he can
> **parry on another party member's behalf**, stepping into an incoming attack
> aimed at an ally. Tighter window than a self-parry; higher payoff.
>
> This makes Amos *essential rather than tanky*, gives the player a genuine
> "who do I save" decision mid-round, and turns the defensive layer into a **team**
> mechanic instead of a solo reflex test.

**Arc — the mask cracks in Q2. `DECIDED`**
> [!danger] Amos infects Echo
> During a **second-quarter encounter with the Cloaked One**, Amos — performing
> heroism, playing the big damn hero — **accidentally knocks Echo into a temporal
> anomaly.** Echo is infected. Terminally.
>
> **This is what's under the mask, and this is what breaks it.** The bravado stops
> being a charming flaw the instant it puts a fatal illness in a friend's body. He
> was performing heroism and the performance had a cost he didn't pay.
>
> Note the elegance: the villain creates the *situation*; **Amos does the damage.**
> The Cloaked One doesn't even have to aim.

> [!tip] The cruelest version — recommended
> **Nobody blames him.** Echo forgives him, maybe instantly, maybe without ever
> landing a word of anger. The party calls it an accident, because it *was* one.
> Amos is left holding a guilt no one will relieve him of by accusing him of
> anything. **Unforgiven because unaccused.**

## 5 — Lyra (temp)
**Dancer — Ranged DPS / Support · chakrams · idol**
*Reference: Dancer (FFXIV).* `PROPOSAL: name.` Canon: a member of the k-pop-inspired
girl group performing at the festival.

**One-line:** A performer whose whole life is a stage persona — standing next to a
prince whose whole life is a mask.

**Backstory `PROPOSAL`.** Two names: her **stage name** and the one her family uses.
Which one the party calls her is a character beat. She's on stage when the
explosion hits — the crowd she was performing to becomes the casualty list.
`TODO:` name, group name, and whether the other members survive.

**Combat identity.** Ranged physical DPS plus buffs/debuffs.
- **Action command:** *throw + catch* — **two windows per action.** Land the throw
  for damage; land the catch to keep the buff/tempo. Missing the catch costs the
  rider, not the hit. Chakrams are the only weapon in the roster that comes back.
- **Dance:** **DDR-like rhythm sequences** (vocabulary type #5) that buff an ally —
  a "partner" she's tethered to. **Keep them short — four arrows, not sixteen**; the
  ~1–2 s time budget applies (→ [[02 - Combat Design]] §3.0).

**Arc `PROPOSAL`.** She and **Amos** are the party's two performers. One masks with
volume, one with choreography. `TODO:` do they recognize it in each other?

## 6 — Vesper (temp)
**Elemental Mage · crystal focus** `PROPOSAL`
**Fills:** the elemental slot — **and the twin-city gap.** Nobody in the party is
from the dark city.

**One-line:** A foundry mage from the other half of the world, who thinks magic is
just engineering with worse documentation.

**Backstory `PROPOSAL`.** Raised in **Darkrai (temp)** — the city of darkness —
working a **crystal foundry**. To Vesper, elemental magic isn't mystic; it's
*industrial*. You channel a crystal, you respect the tolerances, you don't
overcharge the line. Blue-collar, practical, allergic to Amos's entire personality.
Their presence drags the **twin-city relationship** into the party as a daily
friction instead of a map label.

**Combat identity.**
- **Action command:** *charge & release* — hold to build power, release inside the
  window. **Overcharge backfires** on the caster. The party's clearest risk/reward
  dial, and thematically it's just a foundry safety rule.

**Why this character.** Clarus vs. Darkrai (temp) is currently lore. This makes it
**dialogue**. `TODO:` element specialization or full spread?

## 7 — Echo (temp)
**Non-elemental Mage — force, sound, psyche** `PROPOSAL`
**Fills:** the non-elemental slot — **and the party's control group** for Cam's condition.

> [!danger] Canon-level, not a character detail. Read before vetoing.
> **Echo joins healthy and becomes infected during the journey.** They develop
> **textbook Temporal Sickness** — full green irises, temporal tics — in front of
> the party, in real time.

**Why this matters — Echo is the control group.**
Cam and Klaus **already know** Cam's condition isn't Temporal Sickness (see
[[05 - World Bible]] § Cam's symptoms). The two conditions look nothing alike:
Echo's eyes go **fully green** with visible temporal tics; Cam just looks
**exhausted** — dark circles, slow to respond, worn out after fights, irises only
**creeping**.

So Echo isn't a false mirror the party misreads. Echo is **proof.** Klaus — a
researcher — now has a **textbook case and an anomaly at the same campfire, and
they don't match.** An N of 2 that contradicts itself is a scientist's nightmare
and a superb character engine. The party doesn't get *told* Cam is different; they
**watch the difference happen.**

**Two people dying of the same color, for opposite reasons.** One was injured by
the enemy. One was chosen by the goddesses. One can be saved by the Undo — and one
**is** the Undo.

**Backstory `PROPOSAL`.** Word-name in the pattern of **Whisper**. Pre-infection he
is a clean non-elemental mage — force, sound, psyche. Post-infection his **mind
loops**: lost thoughts, re-lived moments, sentences repeated. His name turns out to
have been an omen.
`TODO:` are word-names **given by diviners** in this culture? Diviners read fate
(**Kai**'s domain) — which would make Echo's name a *prophecy*, and tie the naming
convention to the tarot motif.

**Combat identity.**
- **Pre-infection:** a clean, controlled non-elemental caster.
- **Post-infection — *repeating shot* `CANON`:** the disease **unlocks** it. Spells
  echo — repeat for additional hits.
- **Action command:** *hold the loop* — Echo fighting their own condition to keep a
  repeat steady. **Their sickness is their mechanic.**

> [!question] The moral wrinkle this creates
> **The disease makes Echo stronger.** Two party members now carry a power that is
> killing them — Cam's from the goddesses, Echo's from the enemy. Symmetry.
>
> And the Undo **takes the repeating shot away.** In Timeline B, Echo was never
> infected: healthy, ordinary, weaker, and entirely unaware. Healed by an act
> they'll never know happened, performed by a friend they never met.

**Arc — the infection. `DECIDED`**
- **Joins early** — the player gets to know Echo **healthy** first. The loss only
  lands if there was something to lose.
- **Infected in the second quarter**, during an encounter with the **Cloaked One**
  — **after Amos joins.** *(See [[06 - Characters]] § Amos.)*
- **Amos knocks him into a temporal anomaly.** Not villainy. **Vanity.**
- The infection **unlocks the repeating shot**: Amos handed him a death sentence
  and a power in the same motion. Which puts Echo in **the same shape as Cam** —
  power that kills you — except Cam's came from goddesses and Echo's came from a
  friend showing off.

`TODO:` Echo's pronouns — creator used **he/him**; adopted here.
`TODO:` does Echo blame Amos? *(The recommended answer is no, and that it's worse.)*

## 8 — Magpie (temp)
**Rogue / Thief · daggers · avian-folk** `PROPOSAL`
**Fills:** the rogue slot — **and gives the tome hunt an engine.**

**One-line:** The reason the party can find eight scattered tomes without the plot
handing them over.

**Backstory `PROPOSAL`.** Avian-folk (extending the beast-folk canon past canine
and feline), word-name in the **Whisper/Echo** pattern. A treasure hunter who has
spent years chasing **tome rumors** — not out of destiny, but because they're the
most valuable objects on the planet. **The party's tome-finding is Magpie's job**,
which means the collection hook has a *character* driving it rather than a quest
marker.

**Combat identity.**
- **Action command:** *flurry + steal window* — rapid multi-press for a dagger
  flurry, with a separate, tighter window to **steal**. Out of battle: locks,
  routes, rumor networks.

**Why this character.** The **8 tomes** are a collection mechanic tied to the *true
ending*. Without someone whose whole deal is finding them, that hunt is a checklist.
`TODO:` do they want to sell the tomes? A thief who learns what fusion *did* to the
planet is a natural arc.

## 9 — Vale (temp)
**Gunslinger · firearms + ammo** `PROPOSAL`
**Fills:** the gun gap — **and the "who enforces the ban?" faction gap.**

> [!success] The most useful slot in the roster
> **Vale is a former enforcer of the time-magic ban.**
>
> [[05 - World Bible]] flags an open question: *who banned time magic, and who
> enforces it?* Answer: an institution — and Vale used to work for it. Which means
> the party is travelling with a person whose **entire career was hunting people
> like Cam**, and who now walks beside the largest violation of that ban alive.
>
> That's permanent, load-bearing conflict that costs nothing to maintain. Every
> time Cam rewinds, Vale watches. And the ban is **reasonable** — she isn't wrong.

**Backstory `PROPOSAL`.** Older than the university kids; the party's veteran and
its tonal counterweight to a cast of students, idols, and princes. Left the service
`TODO: why?` — the interesting version is that she saw the ban enforced on someone
who didn't deserve it, and the *rules were still right*.

**Combat identity.**
- **Action command:** *active reload* — a tight sweet-spot window on reload.
  **Perfect reload = bonus damage; miss = jam and lose the turn.** The highest
  risk/reward in the party, and the only character where **ammo is a real
  resource**. (Reference: Gears of War's active reload — the definitive gun-timing
  mechanic.)

**Arc `PROPOSAL` — corrected under the anomaly ruling.**
> [!note] The law was **right**. It was **insufficient**.
> Anomalies are **baseline** — genuinely real, genuinely predating the villain (see
> [[05 - World Bible]] § Anomalies). So Vale's career was **not** wasted and the Ban
> was **not** a lie. She enforced a sound law against a real danger.
>
> Her arc is worse than being wrong: she was **right, and it didn't matter.** She
> spent years policing the symptom faithfully while a man quietly made the disease
> worse, and no institution she served was even *looking* for him. The rules were
> correct and the world still got sicker.
>
> That's a harder thing to carry than a wasted career, and it doesn't require the
> Ban to be a fraud.
