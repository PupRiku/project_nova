## Premise

_(draft — for creator approval)_

- **Tone:** industrial/magitech fantasy (the FF6/FF7/FFXII lineage) — a warm,
  lived-in world of crystal-tech, airships, and firearms, shadowed by a slow
  temporal catastrophe. Domestic warmth that curdles into disaster.
- **Hook:** **Cam** can _read and bend time_ — in a world that has outlawed
  exactly that, and where time itself is making people sick. The power that
  makes Cam extraordinary is the same power the world fears, and mastering it
  means uncovering what is truly breaking Novum.

## Thematic spine

Time is the game's subject, its mechanic, and its meaning. The **parry / dodge /
timed-press** combat is diegetically _reading time_; Cam's story is learning to
move from time happening **to** them (the deja-vu loop) to time answering **to**
them (the rewind).

> [!danger] The green thread — protect this
> **Green** is the color of Temporal Sickness (green irises), of banned time
> magic, of the antagonist's cloak, and of the flash when Cam's power triggers.
> Cam's gift literally manifests in the color of the forbidden disease. Keep this
> visual/thematic link deliberate and consistent across writing and art.

## Cosmology & myth

### The Eight Tomes

Eight **guardian creatures** of Novum, bound to eight tomes that hold the key to
summoning them. The tomes can be **fused** — in duos, trios, or all eight.

### Gio, the Creature of Chaos

Fusing **all eight** tomes summons **Gio**. This is the apocalyptic combination.

### Ai and Kai — the goddesses

- **Ai** — goddess of **time**
- **Kai** — goddess of **fate**

## The Cataclysm — "The Split"

Novum was once peaceful and resource-rich. Greed led some to seek the tomes and
**wage war** with the summoned guardians. The fusion property was then unknown —
and during the war, **all eight were accidentally fused**, summoning **Gio**.
Enraged by the war, Gio struck the **center of the planet**, slicing Novum in two.

**Ai and Kai held the pieces together.** Novum barely survived; many perished.
The **centers of both halves** were the only initially-livable areas — these
became the twin cities. Over time Novum healed and grew habitable again; the
**tomes were scattered**. The wound still heals, like a scab, beneath a mountain
range: **Dragon's Spine**.

## Present-day Novum

Mostly at peace, with "problematic foes and factions here and there." A magitech
society: **crystal-based communications**, **airships**, **basic vehicles**,
**guns and ammunition** alongside swords and magic.

**The recent disturbance:** rare **temporal anomalies** — blips; areas frozen in
time, looping, or aging / de-aging rapidly. Popular belief attributes them to Ai
and Kai's healing work. _The game explores what is actually happening._ (Prime
suspect location: the still-healing wound under **Dragon's Spine**.)

## Time magic & the Ban

The practice of **time magic is banned worldwide**. Crucially, this is **not** a
tyrant's decree — it is a **public-health consensus**, agreed upon by all peoples
because they have seen only harm come from the anomalies. The ban is
_reasonable_, which is what makes it dramatically interesting: it is the
understandable thing standing between Novum and understanding its own crisis.

## Temporal Sickness

A known ailment afflicting those "infected" by temporal phenomena.

- **Signature marker:** **green irises** — visible, feared, socially charged.
- **Symptom behavior:** bodily processes **loop**. (Canon example: a heat-stroke
  victim whose _sweating/cooling_ resets endlessly, requiring an IV to offset
  fluid loss — see the festival medic-tent scene in the Chapter 1 story notes.)

## The Tarot motif

Divination is **real** in Novum. It recurs as narrative device, enemy taxonomy,
and art language:

- **The Tower (Upright)** — refuses to leave Cam's future card across repeated
  readings; read as an **omen** of the coming disaster.
- **The Fool** — the **first boss**, a monster erupted from a thrown card.
- **2 of Swords** — the festival's tutorial enemies.
- The antagonist **weaponizes cards** (throws a card that becomes a monster).

> [!note] Design leverage + scope
> The 78-card deck is a ready-made bestiary/boss framework and visual language.
> **Scope flag (for GDD):** decide how many arcana actually become content vs.
> flavor — this can balloon.
