## Two decks, three states `DECIDED`

> [!important] There are **two physical decks** and **three art states.**
> **Vaik's deck *is* Esmerelda's deck** — the same object, corrupted. He stole it
> after draining her, and his power soured it.

| | **Esmerelda's deck** | | **Whisper's deck** |
|---|---|---|---|
| | **Classic** *(before)* | **Corrupted** *(after Vaik)* | **Modern** |
| **Style** | classic / Rider-Waite lineage | Persona 5-adjacent, but *less pop-art*; stylized menace | **oil-painted**, dreamy — ref: **[The Fountain Tarot](https://www.fountaintarot.com/)** |
| **Owner** | Esmerelda | **Vaik** — stolen | **Whisper's own**, throughout |
| **Used for** | readings — **flashback only** | **summoning creatures** — the cards he throws | **readings** |
| **Appears as** | Vaik's reading in exile | enemies & bosses | spreads |
| **Carries a face?** | **no** — iconographic | **no** — distorted | **YES** — the only one that can |

> [!danger] The deck is a **victim**
> It was beautiful and he made it a weapon. **It is the only thing he stole that he
> still uses every day.** The flashback shows it **classic**; the present shows it
> **corrupted** — the player is watching **one object degrade**, not comparing two.

> [!success] Production consequence — this is **cheaper**
> The corrupted cards are **not 20 independent designs.** They are the **same
> compositions, soured** — same figures, same staging, same iconography, run through
> a corruption treatment. A **shared design language**, not a second deck from
> scratch. It also makes the flashback→present read instantly.

> [!success] The art style **is** the mechanism — no lore explanation needed
> Why can Esmerelda's likeness appear on **Whisper's** Star and nowhere else?
> Because of what each medium can physically do:
> - **Classic** is **iconographic** — flat, symbolic. A *Star*, not a woman. It does
>   not do portraiture.
> - **Corrupted** is **distorted** — a face in that style is a mask, not a person.
> - **Modern** is an **oil painting.** It is the only one of the three where a real
>   human face can live.
>
> **Esmerelda's own deck could never have shown her face — it isn't that kind of
> art. Whisper's can.** That's why the catch exists only there, and it costs zero
> lore.

> [!note] The reference, accurately
> The Fountain Tarot is **79 silver-gilded cards** reproducing **original oil
> paintings** by Jonathan Saiz — **fresh, intuitive interpretations of the classic
> Rider-Waite**, self-described as *dreamy, direct, beautiful.* The important part
> for us: it is a **reinterpretation of the same iconography**, which is exactly why
> both decks can be the same archetypes in different registers.
>
> *(It also has a **79th card** — "The Fountain" — with no standard-deck equivalent.
> **A card that exists only in Whisper's deck** is sitting there if it's ever
> wanted.)*

## The deck's fate — it differs by ending `DECIDED`

> [!success] **Path B (Undo):** she never learns.
> Vaik never existed → Esmerelda was never drained → **the deck was never stolen or
> soured.** It simply passes down: four generations, properly, to a
> great-granddaughter who would own **both** — her own modern deck and the classic
> one that never left the family. **The deck comes home in the timeline where none
> of this happened, and nobody ever knows it was gone.**

> [!danger] **Path A (Restraint):** she learns — and she **cleanses the cards.**
> After the final battle, Whisper recovers Esmerelda's deck and **spends a long time
> cleansing it.** Card by card. Slowly.

> [!important] Whisper and the deck have **the same arc** — protect this
> Both were beautiful. Both were **taken by Vaik**. Both were left broken in a way
> that looked permanent. And both come back **only through slow, unglamorous,
> patient work that nobody watches.**
>
> **She cleanses the cards the way she rebuilt her sight** — badly at first,
> frustrated, crying, one at a time, for a long time. **She is the only person alive
> qualified to do it**, and not because she's a diviner: because she has spent the
> whole game doing exactly this to herself.

> [!success] This gives **Path A its image** — which it badly needed
> **Path B's ending is a god's ending:** one act; everything fixed; instantly;
> forever.
> **Path A's ending is a woman sitting on the floor with a damaged deck, cleaning it
> card by card.**
>
> Not triumph — **maintenance.** The timeline where nothing is fixed *for* you,
> everyone has to do the work, and **the work is the point.**
>
> Path B has no category for this. **You cannot cleanse a deck that was never
> stolen.**

## `PROPOSAL` — The Fool was Vaik's own card

Esmerelda's reading told Vaik of **new beginnings**. **The Fool (0)** *is* the card
of new beginnings — the first card in the deck, the leap into the unknown.

And the first creature **Vaik summons at the festival is The Fool.**

> [!tip] If The Fool was the card she dealt him, then he throws his own reading at a
> boy on a lawn.
> The prophecy he scoffed at, weaponized. He didn't just steal her deck — he took
> the card that promised him a beginning and made it a monster. **Every time he
> throws it, he's spitting on her.**
>
> Costs nothing; both halves are already in the script. Confirm or reject.

## Art direction constraint `IMPORTANT`

> [!warning] Card art budget — **~40 unique illustrations across 3 styles**
> | State | Cards needed | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Classic** | ~5 | flashback readings only |
> | **Corrupted** | ~15–20 | bosses + thrown cards — **same compositions as Classic, soured** |
> | **Modern** | ~13 | Cam's readings (~9 unique) + Whisper's 3 + The Star |
>
> A real art line item, not a footnote — **but cheaper than it looks**: Classic and
> Corrupted are **one deck in two states**, sharing composition and iconography.
> → [[01 - Game Design Document]] §6.

> [!warning] **Esmerelda's design must survive 20+ hours of player memory.**
> The payoff requires a player to see her in a **flashback**, then recognize her on
> a **card** many hours later — unprompted, with no dialogue pointing at it.
>
> Consequences for [[04 - Art & Audio Direction]]:
> - Esmerelda needs a **distinctive, memorable silhouette/face** — not a generic old
>   fortune teller.
> - **The Star's art must be legible** at whatever size the card is displayed.
> - The flashback must give the player a **clear, lingering look** at her.
> - **Ordering:** the Vaik flashback **must** precede Whisper's return scene.

## Rules for adding cards

1. **Log it here first.** Card, where, which deck.
2. **Never *Whisper's* Star.** (Vaik's and Esmerelda's Stars are free — different
   art, and they cannot carry a face.)
3. Readings are **characterization** — pick cards that mean something about the
   person being read.
4. **Upright vs. reversed is canon**, not flavor. Record it.
5. If a card appears in **both** decks, note it — the art differs, the meaning may
   not.
